Proud to share that our Remote Sensing research group at the Multidisciplinary AI Research Centre (MARC), University of Peradeniya, has a new paper accepted for publication in **IEEE JSTARS** (Q1, SCIE; Impact Factor: 5.3).

Our work, **"Preprocessing Algorithm Leveraging Geometric Modeling for Scale Correction in Hyperspectral Images for Improved Unmixing Performance"**, tackles a fundamental bottleneck in remote sensing and computer vision.

## The Problem

Hyperspectral cameras capture incredibly detailed spectral data, but natural variations in illumination and surface topography create massive scale distortions in the pixel signatures. These large-scale distortions often overwhelm even the most advanced AI models, confusing the optimization processes and leading to highly inaccurate material estimations.

## The Solution & Impact

Instead of building overly complex, computationally heavy neural networks to guess through the noise, we developed a novel unsupervised preprocessing algorithm. By utilizing a rigorous mathematical framework to restore the fundamental simplex geometry of the data, our algorithm acts like a corrective lens—fixing scale-induced variability before it is ever fed into the unmixing models.

The results? Our preprocessing step consistently achieved abundance estimation error reductions of around 50% across a wide range of state-of-the-art algorithms. This makes existing pipelines vastly more accurate for real-world Geographic Information System (GIS) applications like mineral mapping, precision agriculture, and environmental monitoring.
